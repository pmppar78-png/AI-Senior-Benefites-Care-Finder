Sources & Research Methodology

This is the list of sources the guides on Senior Benefits Care Finder actually use, with what each one is used for. It only lists sources that pages cite.

Updated: November 15, 2026 Methodology
Important: This page is for informational purposes only. It does not constitute financial, legal, tax, medical, or insurance advice. Always confirm details with official program representatives and licensed professionals before making decisions. Data may change -- verify current figures with official sources.

Government Sources

  • CMS and Medicare.gov – Medicare premiums and deductibles (2026 Parts A & B fact sheet), Part D costs including the $2,100 2026 cap, Extra Help and Medicare Savings Program limits, plan landscape data, and Medicaid spousal-impoverishment bulletins.
  • Social Security Administration – the 2026 COLA and SSI payment rates, and average benefits by state from SSA’s annual statistical tables.
  • USDA Food and Nutrition Service – SNAP income limits and benefit amounts (FY2027), the broad-based categorical eligibility chart, and the state SNAP agency directory.
  • Department of Veterans Affairs – pension and Aid & Attendance rates (effective December 1, 2025).
  • HHS – the 2026 poverty guidelines.
  • Other federal and state sources – LIHEAP (HHS ACF), Lifeline (USAC), the Weatherization Assistance Program (Energy Department), HUD, the Eldercare Locator (ACL), and state agency pages for state programs.

Published Data and Research

  • CareScout (formerly Genworth) 2025 Cost of Care Survey – monthly medians for assisted living, nursing homes, home care and adult day care, by metro area, state and nationally.
  • American Association for Long-Term Care Insurance 2026 price index – sample long-term care insurance prices.
  • KFF Medicaid expansion tracker – which states expanded Medicaid.

Earlier versions of this page listed other organizations (such as BLS wage data, NCOA and the AARP Public Policy Institute) as data sources. Their figures are not used in the guides, so they were removed from this list. Some pages may still link to them as further reading.

How Figures Are Labeled

  • Every dollar figure names its year (for example, “2026” or “FY2027”).
  • Local figures say whether they are for the metro area, the state or the nation.
  • When no reliable figure exists, the page says “Not published” or explains that the amount varies, rather than estimating.
  • The figures are kept in one sourced data file, with a source link and retrieval date for each, and the state and city pages are built from it.

Limits

Program rules change, and states and counties apply them differently. A figure here can lag behind a change or not fit a particular household. Always confirm with the agency that runs the program before you act, and see Fact-Checking for how errors are handled.
