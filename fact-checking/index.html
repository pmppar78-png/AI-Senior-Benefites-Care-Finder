Fact-Checking Policy

Seniors and caregivers use this site to make real decisions, so the figures have to be right. This page explains, plainly, how facts are checked on a one-person site, what is not done, and what has been corrected.

Updated: November 15, 2026 Policy
Important: This page is for informational purposes only. It does not constitute financial, legal, tax, medical, or insurance advice. Always confirm details with official program representatives and licensed professionals before making decisions. Data may change -- verify current figures with official sources.

Who Checks the Facts

Senior Benefits Care Finder is researched and written by Paul Paradis alone. There is no editorial staff, no separate fact-checker, and no outside medical, legal or insurance reviewer. AI tools help with drafting and with comparing thousands of pages against the source data, but the sources and changes are reviewed by Paul.

How a Figure Gets Checked

  • Start with the issuing agency. Medicare figures from CMS and Medicare.gov, Social Security and SSI from SSA, SNAP from USDA, VA pension rates from VA.gov, poverty guidelines from HHS. News stories and other benefit sites are not used as sources for figures.
  • Record it once, with its source. Figures go into a single sourced data file with the source link and the date it was retrieved, and the state and city pages are built from that file, so one correction fixes every page that uses it.
  • Label the year and scope. Each figure says which year it applies to, and whether it is a metro, state or national figure.
  • Say when there is no figure. If no reliable local or state number is published, the page says so instead of estimating.

Update Cycle

  • October–November: Medicare premiums, deductibles and Part D limits (CMS) and the Social Security COLA and SSI rates (SSA).
  • October 1: new SNAP income limits and benefit amounts (USDA fiscal year).
  • December 1: new VA pension rates.
  • When published: new CareScout cost of care survey; state program changes such as a state ending broad-based categorical eligibility.

This cycle has not always been kept. In November 2026 a review found pages that were a year or more out of date; they were corrected and are listed in the log below.

When Sources Disagree

The agency that runs the program wins: CMS for Medicare, SSA for Social Security, USDA for SNAP rules and the state agency for how the state applies them. If something cannot be resolved, the page says the figure may vary and links to the agency.

Corrections Log

Significant corrections are listed here with the date they were made.

  • November 15, 2026 – SNAP pages updated to USDA’s FY2027 figures ($306 maximum benefit for one person, $1,330 net income limit), with each state’s broad-based categorical eligibility rule from USDA’s November 2026 chart. Earlier pages showed FY2024 figures and wrongly applied the gross income test to seniors.
  • November 15, 2026 – Part D pages corrected: the coverage gap (“donut hole”) ended in 2025, and 2026 out-of-pocket drug costs are capped at $2,100.
  • November 15, 2026 – Care cost figures that were estimates not tied to a published survey were replaced with CareScout 2025 Cost of Care Survey medians, labeled as metro, state or national figures. Long-term care insurance prices now come from the AALTCI 2026 price index.
  • November 15, 2026 – State program facts corrected (state SSI supplements, state pharmaceutical assistance programs, Social Security taxation), VA Aid & Attendance updated to $2,424/month (rates effective Dec 1, 2025), and source links that pointed nowhere replaced with working official links.
  • November 15, 2026 – Provider and Medicare Advantage city page titles no longer say “Best”, because those pages explain how to find and compare options rather than rank them.
  • November 15, 2026 – The AI guide now works from a dated list of current 2026 figures, after it was found quoting an outdated Part D cap and SNAP limits.

Report an Error

Email contact@seniorbenefitscarefinder.com with the page address, what looks wrong, and a link to the official source if you have one. Paul reads every message, checks it against the source, and fixes verified errors, usually within a few business days.
