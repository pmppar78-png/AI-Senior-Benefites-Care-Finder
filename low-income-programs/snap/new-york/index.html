New York SNAP After 60: Rules, ESAP and Where to Apply

New York uses the federal name, SNAP, for food benefits, and the state Office of Temporary and Disability Assistance (OTDA) supervises the program. Applications are decided locally: by a county Department of Social Services upstate and on Long Island, and by the Human Resources Administration (HRA) in New York City. Older applicants meet one rule set statewide but very different front doors.

USDA's BBCE chart (updated November 12, 2026), the federal FY2027 tables (October 1, 2026–September 30, 2027), New York's ESAP regulation and the myBenefits portal were checked November 15, 2026.

Why most New York retirees are not under BBCE

Broad-based categorical eligibility lets a state relax the gross-income and asset tests. On USDA's BBCE chart, New York has two narrow rows instead of one for every household: households with dependent care expenses qualify at 200% of poverty, and households with earned income qualify at 150%, each with no asset limit. A retired person or couple living on Social Security, a pension or savings, with no paycheck and no dependent-care bills, fits neither row.

Those households are therefore assessed under the ordinary federal rules for a household with someone 60 or older. There is no gross-income ceiling; the household must pass the net-income test after deductions, and the regular federal resource limit applies, which for FY2027 is $4,750 when a member is 60 or older or disabled. Westchester County's SNAP page, for example, asks for proof of bank accounts or other resources when anyone in the home is over 60, which fits that picture.

New York's resource and utility figures are set by OTDA; this guide relies on USDA's chart, the state ESAP regulation and county DSS pages, so confirm any resource rule with your county worker.

Deductions that usually decide an older New Yorker's case

Because the net test is the one that counts, the deductions matter more than the headline income. For a member 60 or older, unreimbursed medical costs above $35 a month come off income: Medicare and supplemental premiums, co-pays, prescriptions, dental work, hearing aids and transport to treatment are common examples. Only the portion over $35 counts, and the household must show proof.

Rent or mortgage, property taxes, insurance and utility costs feed the shelter deduction. The deduction is the part of shelter cost above half of the income left after the other deductions. For a household with a member 60 or older there is no cap on it, unlike the $769 cap for other households in FY2027.

ESAP: a lighter process for households of older adults

New York's Elderly Simplified Application Project is written into state regulation at 18 NYCRR 387.26. It covers households in which every adult is 60 or older and/or disabled and nobody has earned income. Households that qualify for New York's combined SSI-based programs (NYSNIP or NYSCAP), people in group living facilities, and households with a member on ongoing public assistance are excluded.

The rule offers three practical benefits. A shortened application exists, though any SNAP application is screened for ESAP eligibility. Certification can last up to 36 months. And a recertification interview is not required every time; an interview is scheduled only in defined circumstances, and a recertification cannot be denied without first trying to schedule one.

Applying: myBenefits upstate, ACCESS HRA in the city

Outside New York City, myBenefits handles the SNAP application, recertification, change reports and uploads of verification documents, plus EBT balance and PIN tools. Paper applications go to the county DSS. In the five boroughs, HRA's ACCESS HRA portal is the main online route; see the city page for its offices and mail address.

Two current notices on myBenefits matter for older users. Applications to replace stolen SNAP benefits are no longer accepted, so locking the card when it is not in use is the practical defense. The portal also points to electric and gas bill discounts through the state's energy assistance program.

For office addresses, open the pages for New York City, Buffalo and Yonkers.

Frequently asked questions

Is New York's program called SNAP or something else?

It is SNAP. OTDA supervises it statewide, county social services departments run it locally, and in New York City the agency is HRA.

Does a New York retiree face an asset test?

Usually yes. USDA lists New York's BBCE only for households with earned income or dependent care costs, so a retired household is generally reviewed under the federal $4,750 resource limit for members 60 or older.

How long can an ESAP case last in New York?

State regulation allows a certification period of up to 36 months for households in the Elderly Simplified Application Project.
