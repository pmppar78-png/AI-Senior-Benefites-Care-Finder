Virginia SNAP Guide for Adults 60 and Older

The Virginia Department of Social Services (VDSS) sets SNAP policy, and each city or county department of social services handles applications. Older Virginians have seen one notable change in 2026: the combined program for SSI recipients, VaCAP, ended, and those cases are moving to the Elderly Simplified Application Project.

Virginia sources checked November 15, 2026: VDSS, CommonHelp and the September 2026 Change Report; federal references are 7 CFR 273.11(r), USDA's November 12 BBCE chart and FY2027 allotment tables.

The VDSS SNAP page posts this chart, and USDA's BBCE chart confirms that Virginia extends 200% BBCE to all households with no asset limit. An older household above 200% is not finished: under federal rules it can qualify if net income, after deductions, is under 100% of poverty.

VaCAP has ended; ESAP continues

Virginia closed its VaCAP project on June 1, 2026, per VDSS. Former VaCAP households are moved to ESAP and evaluated at their next recertification. A benefit specialist may need to speak with them, and the benefit is no longer a fixed amount: it is now figured from income and allowable expenses, so shelter and medical costs should be reported.

ESAP serves households whose eligible members are all 60 or older with no earnings, and it can certify a case for up to three years (36 months). An ESAP flyer on Virginia's Easy Access portal lists what still has to be reported: a change in who lives in the home, any member starting to earn income, and large lottery or gambling winnings.

Report lottery or gambling winnings of $4,750 or more in a single game. Federal rule 7 CFR 273.11(r)(2)(i) ties this threshold to the elderly/disabled resource limit, which is $4,750 for FY2027; VDSS's Change Report (rev. 9/2026) uses $4,750, while older VDSS pages and the ESAP flyer still show $4,500 or $4,250.

A $200 medical deduction without itemizing

Virginia uses a standard medical deduction. The VDSS SNAP manual (Transmittal 36) sets the medical standard at $235: a household that verifies more than $35 a month in allowable medical costs for an eligible member receives a $200 deduction. A household spending more than $235 can opt out and claim its actual verified costs instead.

Eligible costs named by VDSS include health insurance and Medicare premiums, medical and dental care, transportation to treatment and the cost of a home health aide. The standard means someone with $60 in monthly co-pays receives the same $200 deduction as someone with $230.

Applying and protecting the card

Apply through CommonHelp, which also tracks status, accepts uploaded documents and takes change reports; VDSS lists 833-522-5582 for phone help, and local agencies give 855-635-4370 for applications. A paper application can go to the local department of social services.

Beginning November 1, 2026, sweetened carbonated beverages can no longer be bought with SNAP in Virginia; the benefit amount does not change. Cardholders can lock their EBT card through the ConnectEBT app. Local offices for Virginia Beach, Chesapeake and Richmond are on the city pages.

Richmond's description of SNAPET, Virginia's employment and training program for SNAP, says it targets single adults living alone and refers registrants at application and every 12 months unless an exemption changes. Most applicants over 60 are not the program's focus, but read any referral letter rather than ignoring it.

Deductions and the household behind the chart

Start the Virginia budget by taking $217 off gross monthly income for a household of one, two or three in FY2027. Shelter is calculated separately: after other deductions, only costs above half of the remaining income qualify, but a household with a member 60 or older is not subject to the usual $769 shelter cap. Rent, mortgage payments, property taxes and qualifying utilities are therefore important even for an applicant whose Social Security looks too high at first glance.

Living at one address does not necessarily mean sharing one SNAP budget. Federal rules focus on buying and preparing food together, while spouses living together must be included together. Describe the arrangement accurately at the interview. If the written decision appears to omit an expense that was verified, ask the local department for the calculation and follow the appeal instructions on the notice; federal guidance permits a fair-hearing request within 90 days.

Frequently asked questions

What happened to VaCAP in Virginia?

VDSS ended VaCAP effective June 1, 2026. Those cases are reviewed under ESAP at the next recertification, and the benefit is now calculated from income and expenses.

How much medical expense does Virginia deduct?

If an eligible member has more than $35 a month in verified costs, the standard deduction is $200; households above $235 can claim actual costs instead.

Which Virginia gross limit applies?

VDSS lists 200% of poverty under BBCE for FY2027: $2,660 for one person and $3,608 for two.
