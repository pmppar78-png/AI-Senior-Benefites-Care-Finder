Florida SNAP for Older Adults After SUNCAP

For Florida residents who used to receive food assistance through SUNCAP, the immediate issue is a program transition, not just an income table. Florida DCF says SUNCAP ended September 30, 2026. Its Office of Economic Self-Sufficiency now directs applicants to regular SNAP, including people who receive SSI.

Florida DCF pages and Appendix A-1 were consulted November 15, 2026. The financial table is effective October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2027.

What former SUNCAP recipients need to do

According to DCF's SNAP page, the Social Security Administration no longer determines food-assistance eligibility for Florida SSI households through SUNCAP. During the October 2026–March 2027 phase-out, existing SUNCAP recipients are to receive a DCF notice explaining how to continue food assistance if eligible.

Watch for that notice and act on its instructions; this is not a promise that an old case will renew automatically. The recommended application channel is MyACCESS. A Family Resource Center, a community partner or a paper form sent by mail or fax are alternatives. Receiving SSI does not by itself tell you what action DCF needs during the transition.

Someone already on regular SNAP should follow the notice on that case rather than treating the SUNCAP announcement as a new application requirement. Keep copies of notices and confirmation of anything submitted online.

The Florida income and asset rules

Florida appears on USDA's November 12, 2026 chart with no asset limit and a 200% gross-income threshold under broad-based categorical eligibility. The qualifying non-cash service is described on an ACCESS Florida notice. This is why a bank account or a vehicle does not automatically prevent most Florida households from receiving SNAP.

The chart is a BBCE rule, not a universal gross ceiling on older people. USDA says a household outside BBCE with an elderly or disabled member can instead be evaluated under the regular net-income and resource tests without a gross-income test. Where an asset test applies, such as a household with a disqualified member, DCF's Appendix A-1, effective October 1, 2026, sets $4,750 with an elderly or disabled member and $3,000 otherwise. DCF's eligibility webpage still showed the older $4,500 figure on November 15, 2026; the $4,750 amount is the current one. Ask the worker to explain any exception applied to your household.

Expenses affect the amount, not just eligibility

Florida's FY2027 appendix lists a standard deduction of $217 for one to three household members. Additional allowable deductions can include dependent care, qualifying support payments, earned income and certain medical costs. For an elderly or disabled member, unreimbursed medical costs above $35 monthly may be deductible. Provide premium statements, pharmacy histories and other evidence instead of assuming DCF already knows what Medicare costs you.

Shelter costs are considered using a separate formula, and the excess-shelter deduction is uncapped for a household with an elderly or disabled person. That protection can matter for a renter with high housing costs, but it does not make the benefit equal to the rent bill. Benefits are calculated from the relevant maximum minus approximately 30% of the household's net income, subject to SNAP rounding and minimum-allotment rules.

When several adults share housing, tell DCF who purchases and prepares food together. Federal rules usually require spouses together and include a child under 22 living with a parent, even if they say they eat separately. The food household can otherwise differ from the list of everyone who sleeps in the home.

Finding help without surrendering the case to a partner

DCF's Family Resource Center directory provides county filters and appointment information. The Community Partner Network distinguishes self-service sites that offer computers from staffed partners that help navigate the application. DCF expressly says partners do not decide eligibility: that remains the state agency's job.

Ask whether a site offers hands-on application help or only a computer before arranging transportation. Local guides describe the available offices in Jacksonville, Miami and Tampa. A helper can assist with forms, but written authorization is needed if the person will represent the household rather than simply sit beside you.

File promptly if food is running out. Federal SNAP procedures require screening for expedited service; one qualifying situation is gross monthly income below $150 and liquid resources below $100. Report the immediate circumstances instead of waiting to collect every verification. For a disputed DCF eligibility decision, request the fair hearing described in the notice; USDA sets a 90-day request window.

Shopping rules changed in April 2026

Florida's Healthy SNAP announcement says that from April 20, 2026, SNAP cannot pay for soda, energy drinks, candy or ultra-processed shelf-stable prepared desserts in Florida. These state restrictions are in addition to the usual exclusions for alcohol, tobacco, medicines and nonfood products. A general national shopping list can therefore be misleading at a Florida checkout.

SNAP still helps buy eligible grocery foods, including fruit, vegetables, meat, dairy and food-producing plants or seeds. DCF also explains online grocery purchasing and Fresh Access Bucks, a produce incentive at participating outlets. Delivery charges cannot be covered by the EBT food balance. Keep another payment method available for fees and ineligible items so an online order does not fail at payment.

Frequently asked questions

Will Florida SUNCAP automatically continue in 2027?

DCF says the program ended in September 2026, with notices to existing recipients during a phase-out through March 2027. Follow your notice about regular SNAP rather than assuming SSI will keep the old food-assistance arrangement active.

Why does a Florida store reject candy on my EBT food balance?

The state's food restrictions took effect April 20, 2026 and include candy. Other groceries in the same basket may remain eligible; use a different payment method for a restricted product.
