Using DCF Service Centers for SNAP in Miami

Miami-Dade has more DCF Family Resource Centers in the state directory than Duval or Hillsborough. That gives older residents a choice among sites, though not every service is available without an appointment.

Miami-Dade center listings came from DCF's directory on November 15, 2026.

Miami-Dade DCF centers

DCF's Family Resource Center directory lists three Miami sites: Rohde Service Center, 401 NW 2nd Avenue, Miami, FL 33128; West Kendall Service Center, 12985 SW 130th Court, Miami, FL 33186; and West Dade Service Center, 9766 SW 24th Street, Suite 35, Miami, FL 33165.

It also lists Opa-Locka Service Center at 200 Opa-Locka Boulevard, Opa Locka, FL 33054 and Florida City Service Center at 755 West Palm Drive, Florida City, FL 33034. Several listings give verification fax (305) 377-5544.

What a Miami visit can accomplish

At these centers, DCF allows walk-in document drop-off, identity authentication, computer access to apply and pickup of forms. Use the DCF appointment system for other services. Applying first through MyACCESS can make a center visit shorter.

Older Miami households should apply Florida's statewide SUNCAP transition and shopping rules, summarized in the Florida guide.

Miami aging support and choosing a partner

The Alliance for Aging lists an Elder Helpline at 305-670-HELP, with weekday office hours of 8 a.m. until 5 p.m. Its services include information and referral, home and community-based support, caregiver help and Medicare counseling through SHINE. A person helping a parent can ask about the broader care situation as well as finding food support.

For help actually navigating the state benefits system, DCF's community partner explanation distinguishes a computer-only self-serve site from a staffed partner. Ask which kind of assistance is available: being given a workstation may not be enough for someone with vision, dexterity or language barriers.

Even when a partner helps with the online form, the case stays with DCF. Keep any submission confirmation from MyACCESS, and direct questions about an official request for verification to DCF rather than assuming the partner can extend the due date.

Miami-Dade meals for older residents

Miami-Dade County's Meals for the Elderly program has two routes. Meals on Wheels serves homebound residents 60 and older with seven free frozen meals a week, plus fresh fruit and milk, delivered on weekdays. Spouses qualify regardless of age. Call 786-469-4707 to check eligibility.

Residents who are not homebound can eat at 19 senior-center locations, which serve free meals Monday through Friday from 8 a.m. to 2 p.m. Jack Orr, 550 NW 5th Street, Miami, FL 33128, is one of the downtown sites.

Worked example: high Miami rent and the shelter deduction

For most households the excess shelter deduction stops at $769, but USDA lifts that ceiling when a member is elderly or disabled. Take a 69-year-old with $1,217 in Social Security. After the $217 standard deduction, adjusted income is $1,000. If rent plus the utility amount DCF allows comes to $1,400, everything above half of adjusted income, $500, counts: a $900 deduction.

Net income drops to $100. Thirty percent of that is $30, so the one-person allotment would be $306 minus $30, or $276. A younger applicant with the same numbers would be held to the $769 cap.

Frequently asked questions

Which DCF centers are in Miami itself?

DCF lists Rohde, West Kendall and West Dade service centers with Miami addresses.

Do Miami DCF centers require appointments?

Some tasks are walk-in, including document drop-off and ID authentication. Everything else is reserved for scheduled visits.

Is the SNAP shelter deduction capped for Miami seniors?

No. USDA says households with an elderly or disabled member can deduct all shelter costs above half of their income; the $769 cap applies to other households.
