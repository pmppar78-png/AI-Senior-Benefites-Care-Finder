SNAP in Illinois for People 60 and Older

In Illinois, SNAP benefits arrive on the Illinois Link Card and the Department of Human Services (IDHS) runs the program through Family Community Resource Centers. Two income lines appear on state and federal pages, and an older household should understand both before deciding whether to apply.

IDHS program and office pages, ABE notices, USDA's BBCE chart (updated November 12, 2026) and FY2027 federal tables were read November 15, 2026.

165% on USDA's chart, 200% on the IDHS page

USDA's BBCE chart lists Illinois as extending broad-based categorical eligibility to all households at 165% of poverty with no asset limit. For FY2027 that works out to $2,195 for one person and $2,976 for two, matching the 165% table USDA publishes.

The IDHS SNAP page adds a second table for households with someone 60 or older or disabled, set at 200% of poverty: $2,608 for one and $3,525 for two. That table is labeled "Updated 10/1/2025", so it reflects the previous federal year. At the time of reading IDHS had not posted FY2027 figures for it; doubling USDA's FY2027 net-income limits ($1,330 and $1,804) gives about $2,660 and $3,608. IDHS works from annual poverty guidelines, so its two-person table may show about $3,607, a dollar lower than twice the rounded monthly net limit.

Neither line is a hard cutoff for an older applicant. Under regular federal rules, a household with a member 60 or older skips the gross test and is judged on net income and resources instead.

Social Security, pensions and disability payments all count as income. Against that, an older household can subtract the standard deduction, out-of-pocket medical costs beyond the first $35 a month, and shelter costs that exceed half of its remaining income. Keep pharmacy printouts, premium notices and the lease: the caseworker can only allow what is shown.

Applying through ABE and managing the case

ABE (Application for Benefits Eligibility) accepts applications for SNAP, medical and cash help, and its Manage My Case tool shows benefits, renewal dates and notices. Two ABE notices are current: beginning October 30, 2026, only one Manage My Case account is allowed per household, and ABE is unavailable on Sundays from 6:00 to 11:00 a.m., with possible slowdowns afterward.

The IDHS Help is Here line, 1-800-843-6154, covers cash, SNAP and medical assistance statewide. IDHS also describes emergency SNAP benefits that are ready in five days or less from the date of application for households in urgent need.

The IDHS page also notes that SNAP recipients may qualify for the Lifeline and Link-Up programs, which help with telephone installation and monthly local phone charges, a useful extra for someone on a fixed income.

Restaurant Meals and in-person help

The IDHS office locator includes a "SNAP Restaurant Meals Program" category alongside Family Community Resource Centers, so a county search can show where that option is offered. Federal rules limit restaurant purchases to eligible older, disabled or homeless SNAP members.

Local guides cover the resource centers and meal programs in Chicago, Aurora and Naperville.

Household, renewal and a notice that seems wrong

Food purchasing and preparation determine the SNAP household, not simply the number of names on an apartment lease. Federal rules require spouses living together to apply together; an older parent who buys and prepares food separately from an adult child may be a different group. A permanently disabled person 60 or older can sometimes qualify as a separate household with a spouse even when unable to cook separately, subject to a limit on the other residents' income.

After approval, keep the IDHS notice: it identifies the certification period and reporting duties. ABE Manage My Case shows the redetermination date, so check it before a move or a long visit away from home. For a decision that appears to omit rent or medical costs, ask the resource center for the budget used. USDA provides a fair-hearing right within 90 days of a disputed SNAP decision; the notice gives the filing route. Keep the dated proof that was submitted, rather than relying on a new calculator estimate to explain the disagreement.

Frequently asked questions

Which Illinois income limit applies to a senior household?

USDA lists 165% of poverty for Illinois BBCE, and IDHS also publishes a 200% table for households with a member 60 or older or disabled. A household above both can still be evaluated on net income under federal rules.

What is the Illinois Link Card?

It is the EBT card on which Illinois deposits SNAP benefits each month; it works like a debit card at most grocery stores.

Can more than one person in my home have an ABE Manage My Case account?

From October 30, 2026, ABE allows only one Manage My Case account per household.
