Georgia SNAP: The Senior Application and the Regular Rules

Georgia offers a dedicated Senior SNAP application, but the age on that form is not the age at which federal elderly-household protections begin. A Georgian in their early sixties should not wait to apply just because the simplified form is aimed at an older group. The Division of Family & Children Services, or DFCS, handles both routes.

Read on November 15, 2026: Georgia DFCS application guidance and USDA's November 12 BBCE chart. Every dollar figure here belongs to federal FY2027, which began October 1, 2026 and ends September 30, 2027.

Who can use Georgia Senior SNAP?

The detailed DFCS Senior SNAP page says every household member must be 65 or older, not working, and living on permanent fixed income such as Social Security, retirement or veterans benefits. Members buy and prepare their food together, and the household still must satisfy SNAP eligibility. Form 298 is available in standard and large-print versions, including Spanish.

Form 298, the Senior SNAP application, is for households in which every member is 65 or older; DFCS's general SNAP page mentions 60, but the dedicated Senior SNAP page sets 65 for the form. The federal elderly rules start earlier. Anyone 60 or older applying on the regular application still gets the elderly medical deduction, the uncapped shelter deduction and the net-income-only test.

Senior SNAP is a simpler administrative process, not a separate pot of money with a larger maximum benefit. It does not let an applicant omit income or ignore renewal paperwork. DFCS describes a phone interview with the applicant, another knowledgeable adult household member or an authorized representative.

How Georgia's categorical eligibility works

On USDA's chart dated November 12, 2026, Georgia provides a TANF Community Outreach Services brochure to confer BBCE with no asset limit and a 130% gross-income threshold. This makes the asset treatment more generous for households qualifying through that route than the ordinary federal savings test.

Outside the BBCE group, a household with a person 60 or older or disabled may still qualify using the regular net-income test without a gross-income test. Regular federal rules ordinarily count up to $4,750 in resources for that household in FY2027, with exemptions including the residence and specified retirement accounts. If everyone receives SSI or TANF, federal categorical rules can remove that resource test. Ask DFCS to consider the appropriate route rather than rejecting yourself because of a savings balance or gross pension amount.

Medical and shelter costs can change the result

A fixed income does not mean a fixed benefit award. USDA allows the $217 standard deduction for households of one to three people in FY2027, and eligible medical spending over $35 each month can be deducted for elderly or disabled members. DFCS specifically asks applicants to list medical costs and verify them, including health-insurance premiums, prescriptions, dental care, hearing aids and qualifying transportation.

List the amount actually paid after insurance, not the provider's original charge. For expenses that fluctuate, take the records to the interview and ask how DFCS will budget them. An older household may also receive an uncapped excess-shelter deduction, based on rent or mortgage and qualifying utility costs above half of its adjusted income.

SNAP then compares the household budget with the maximum allotment; the normal benefit calculation takes roughly 30% of net income away from that maximum. Eligible one- and two-person households have a $25 monthly minimum in FY2027. These are not flat grants for everyone who retires.

Filing, interviewing and renewing in Georgia

Georgia Gateway is the online route for applications, supporting documents and renewals. DFCS advises uploading verification there when possible; those without online access can use their county office. Its general application, Form 297, and Senior SNAP Form 298 are different documents, so check which one fits before printing a large packet.

DFCS considers an application filed when it has the head of household's name, address, date and signature, or the signature of another household member, and reaches the agency. Do not postpone filing just to assemble every medical receipt. During the telephone interview, explain what proof is still being requested and keep any deadline the worker gives.

For renewals, Georgia accepts the required information through Gateway or Form 508. A Senior SNAP approval does not last forever without review. Keep contact details current and read each renewal notice even if the Social Security amount changed only by an annual cost-of-living increase.

Keeping the EBT account safe and the case current

DFCS explains that ConnectEBT lets recipients view deposits, transactions and balances and lock a card between shopping trips. It can block use everywhere or outside Georgia, and an automatic re-lock option helps if the card is unlocked for a purchase. Do not give a helper the PIN just to ask a question about eligibility.

Work requirements deserve individual attention for people in their early sixties. Georgia's current work page discusses ages 16–64 and exemptions; age 60 alone should not be treated as a blanket exemption from every time-limit rule. If you receive a work notice, ask DFCS to evaluate disability, caregiving and other possible exceptions before the deadline.

County starting points are available for Atlanta, Augusta and Macon. For an adverse SNAP decision, the federal fair-hearing window is 90 days; retain the notice and follow its appeal instructions.

Frequently asked questions

Can a 62-year-old Georgian get SNAP without Senior SNAP?

Yes, if otherwise eligible. The simplified Georgia form has its own age criteria, while regular SNAP includes federal protections for households with someone 60 or older. Apply under the regular process rather than waiting for your sixty-fifth birthday.

Does Georgia require me to close my savings account?

No such step is required simply to apply. The USDA chart lists no asset ceiling for Georgia's 130% BBCE route, while a household considered under regular federal rules may face a resource test. Let DFCS classify the case before moving or spending savings.
