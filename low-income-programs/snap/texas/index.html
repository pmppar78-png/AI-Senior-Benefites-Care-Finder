Texas SNAP for Older Adults: TSAP and the Lone Star Card

A Texas retiree may be able to use a shorter application and keep a longer certification period through the Texas Simplified Application Project. TSAP changes the paperwork, not the federal food-benefit calculation. The Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC) decides eligibility, while the Lone Star Card carries the approved benefit.

Texas HHSC guidance and USDA standards opened November 15, 2026; FY2027 figures apply October 1, 2026–September 30, 2027, and BBCE details are from USDA's Nov. 12 chart.

When a household fits TSAP

HHSC's Texas Simplified Application Project guidance identifies three conditions: everyone in the food household is at least 60 or receives disability payments; nobody has earned income from work; and nobody receives SNAP-Combined Application Project benefits, known as SNAP-CAP. A mix of qualifying older and disabled members can fit.

The TSAP form is shorter and eligible recipients receive a three-year certification rather than the usual six-month period described on the HHSC page. The agency also says an eligible TSAP household does not need an interview at renewal. This is not a guarantee of an unchanged benefit amount for three years: income and household changes still matter, and official notices still require attention.

For people already receiving SNAP, HHSC evaluates TSAP eligibility at renewal. Those applying for the first time can use Your Texas Benefits or request a paper application through a local HHS benefits office. HHSC asks applicants to have recent medical or pharmacy statements, retirement or disability award letters and bank statements available.

Texas has an asset test even under BBCE

Unlike many states, Texas has not eliminated its asset test through broad-based categorical eligibility. The USDA chart updated November 12, 2026 lists Texas at 165% gross income with a $5,000 asset limit. HHSC's Texas Works Handbook supplies the detail. Under section A-1220, a household is not eligible when countable resources, meaning liquid resources plus excess vehicle value, are over $5,000. Section A-1230 exempts the first $22,500 of the highest-valued countable vehicle and up to $8,700 of each other countable vehicle. USDA's chart summarizes the vehicle rule as $22,000; the handbook figure, from the agency that runs Texas SNAP, is used here.

The categorical connection comes from information about services on the application. USDA states that BBCE cannot reduce eligibility; someone outside that route can still be considered under ordinary SNAP. In the regular route an elderly or disabled household skips the gross-income test but must pass the net-income test, and the handbook's $5,000 resource limit still applies. HHSC decides which exclusions fit; the limit is not a reason to transfer property before applying.

Do not leave deductible bills out of the interview

SNAP is based on a household budget, not Social Security alone. Under USDA's FY2027 standards a one-, two- or three-person household starts with a $217 standard deduction. Medical costs for an elderly or disabled member can lower income further when the allowable, unreimbursed amount exceeds $35 a month. A pharmacy statement may capture recurring costs better than a single receipt.

Give HHSC the Medicare premium notice, insurance information and records of qualifying medical transportation as well. Bills paid by insurance or by someone outside the household are not your deductible spending. The worker can explain which month an expense belongs in and whether additional evidence is needed.

Housing enters the excess-shelter calculation after other deductions; the usual cap does not apply when the household includes an elderly or disabled member. The award generally starts with the household maximum, then subtracts about 30% of net income. A TSAP label does not bypass that calculation or promise the maximum.

Online and local application choices

Your Texas Benefits is the state application channel, not a county food-bank registration. Local HHS offices can provide paper forms, and HHSC describes returning those forms in person, by fax or by mail. Keep proof of the submission so a request for more information can be matched to the right application.

The online account is also useful after an interview, when the agency asks for a clearer pension letter or a missing bank page. Check the deadline in the notice, not a general estimate from a helper. A written authorized representative can assist an applicant who cannot manage the process independently; discuss the extent of that authority with HHSC.

For local support, the Houston guide explains Harris County aging services, San Antonio distinguishes Bexar and Alamo aging agencies, and Dallas describes application navigators. These organizations support applicants; they do not replace HHSC's eligibility decision. USDA allows a hearing request within 90 days if you disagree with a SNAP action.

Using the Lone Star Card under the 2026 restrictions

HHSC says the Lone Star Card works at stores accepting SNAP and can also pay for eligible online groceries. Beginning April 1, 2026, Texas prohibited SNAP purchases of candy and specified sweetened drinks. The drink restriction covers nonalcoholic water-based beverages with 5 grams or more of added sugar or any artificial sweetener; HHSC lists exceptions for milk and qualifying milk substitutes and beverages with more than 50% fruit or vegetable juice.

Those details matter when two drinks look similar on the shelf. Use the current state product guidance rather than assuming every nonalcoholic drink is allowed. Alcohol, tobacco, vitamins, medicines and nonfood products remain outside the SNAP grocery benefit.

Frequently asked questions

Will a small part-time job prevent Texas TSAP?

Earned income is inconsistent with the TSAP criteria HHSC publishes. It does not automatically disqualify the household from regular SNAP; have HHSC evaluate the ordinary application instead.

Is the Texas SNAP asset limit $4,750 for seniors?

No. HHSC's handbook sets a $5,000 countable-resource limit for Texas SNAP households, with vehicle exemptions; the $4,750 figure is the federal default that other states use.
