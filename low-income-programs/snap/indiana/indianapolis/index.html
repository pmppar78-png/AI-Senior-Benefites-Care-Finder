SNAP in Indianapolis for Residents 60 and Older

In Marion County the Division of Family Resources splits its local work across five offices, so the first question for an Indianapolis applicant is which one handles your ZIP code. Most people never need to visit, because the application and most follow-up can happen online, but knowing your office helps when you have paperwork to drop off.

Office and program details were taken from FSSA pages on November 15, 2026; benefit figures are USDA's for October 1, 2026, through September 30, 2027.

Five DFR offices, assigned by ZIP code

FSSA's Marion County office page lists a north, central, east, west and south location, each tied to a set of ZIP codes. A few examples from that list:

Applying from Indianapolis

The FSSA Benefits Portal takes SNAP applications from anywhere in the county, and it is also where you report changes once a case is open. Anyone who would rather hand in paper can take it to the office that covers their ZIP code. A caseworker interview follows either way, and DFR then mails a list of any proof it still needs, with a deadline.

For a retiree, the documents that most often change the benefit amount are the ones showing medical spending. Keep the pharmacy history, the Medicare premium notice and receipts for out-of-pocket care together, because DFR only allows the medical deduction for expenses it can verify.

The numbers Indianapolis households are measured against

Indiana does not use the broad-based categorical eligibility option, so Marion County caseworkers apply the standard federal tests. A household that includes someone 60 or older skips the gross-income test and is judged on net income alone.

Countable assets do not include the house you live in or a car the household uses to get around; FSSA exempts those vehicles whatever they are worth. The Indiana SNAP guide walks through the deductions and the Hoosier Works card schedule in more detail.

Frequently asked questions

Which Indianapolis DFR office do I use?

The one FSSA assigns to your ZIP code. For example, 46227 is served by the south office and 46222 by the central office; the Marion County page lists every ZIP.

Do I have to go to an office to apply in Indianapolis?

No. The FSSA Benefits Portal accepts the application online, and the interview and document requests can be handled without a visit.
