Michigan's Food Assistance Program for Older Adults

Michigan's Department of Health and Human Services (MDHHS) calls SNAP the Food Assistance Program, or FAP, and loads benefits on the Michigan Bridge Card. For older residents, two state choices stand out: most groups face no asset limit, and an eligible senior can use the card at participating restaurants.

MDHHS food assistance, eligibility and Restaurant Meal Program pages, USDA's BBCE chart (updated November 12, 2026) and FY2027 federal tables were reviewed November 15, 2026.

Savings usually do not block a Michigan case

The MDHHS eligibility page states that, effective March 1, 2024, most FAP groups do not have an asset limit. An asset limit returns only when a member is disqualified for an intentional program violation, an employment-and-training sanction (head of household only) or as a fleeing felon. In those groups the limit is $3,000, rising to $4,500 when someone is 60 or older or disabled.

USDA's BBCE chart lists Michigan at 200% of poverty for all households with no limit on assets. Using USDA's FY2027 poverty-level amounts, 200% equals $2,660 a month for one person and $3,608 for two. A household above that can still qualify through the regular senior route, where only net income is tested.

MDHHS counts most earned and unearned income, naming wages, rental income, Social Security and veterans benefits. On the expense side it lists shelter (rent, mortgage, heat, electric, water and telephone), dependent care, court-ordered child support, and medical costs for members who are disabled or at least 60, including dental care, nursing care, supplies and insurance premiums.

Restaurant Meal Program: no extra application

Michigan's Restaurant Meal Program lets certain FAP recipients buy prepared food with the Bridge Card at authorized restaurants. No separate application is needed, but every member of the group must be 60 or older, disabled (receiving disability or blindness payments, or a government disability retirement for a permanent disability), homeless, or the spouse of an eligible recipient. MDHHS publishes a restaurant map and list.

A couple in which one spouse is 67 and the other 58 can therefore qualify, because a spouse of an eligible member is included. A household that also contains an adult grandchild who is none of those things cannot.

Applying through MI Bridges

MI Bridges is where Michigan residents apply for food assistance, check status, manage the account and report changes in income, address or household. MDHHS notes that upgraded chip- and tap-enabled Bridge Cards are coming later in 2026, so keep the mailing address current through "Report Changes". Michigan also runs MiCAP, a combined application project for people receiving SSI.

Federal work-requirement rules changed March 1, 2026, and some adults 18–64 now must show proof of work; most older applicants are outside that age band. MDHHS office details for three cities follow: Detroit, Grand Rapids and Warren.

MDHHS also publishes a notice titled "Home Heating Credit May Affect the Amount of Food Assistance You Receive." A homeowner or renter who files for that credit should read that notice and report the credit if the food assistance notice asks about it.

Who belongs on the application and what follows approval

MDHHS defines a food-assistance group as people who live together and buy and prepare food together. Federal rules make spouses at the same address one household even when they say they eat separately. An older person sharing housing with unrelated adults is not automatically required to include everyone's wages, but the application needs an accurate account of food arrangements.

Once approved, read the certification dates and change-reporting instructions in the MDHHS notice. Use MI Bridges for the renewal or verification steps the notice requests, and keep copies of submitted records. A food benefit and a Meals on Wheels referral can run alongside each other; neither substitutes for keeping the other program's paperwork current.

If the decision appears to have missed a medical premium or used the wrong rent, request an explanation of the budget from the assigned office. Federal SNAP guidance allows a fair-hearing request within 90 days of a decision. That review concerns eligibility and benefit amounts, whereas a lost Bridge Card is a card-service problem.

Frequently asked questions

Does Michigan count savings for a senior FAP case?

For most groups, no. Since March 1, 2024 only groups with certain disqualified members have an asset limit, set at $4,500 when someone is 60 or older or disabled.

Do I need to sign up for Michigan's Restaurant Meal Program?

No. If every member of your FAP group is elderly, disabled, homeless or the spouse of an eligible recipient, you can use the Bridge Card at participating restaurants.

Is FAP the same as SNAP?

Yes. Michigan's Food Assistance Program is the state's name for federal SNAP benefits.
