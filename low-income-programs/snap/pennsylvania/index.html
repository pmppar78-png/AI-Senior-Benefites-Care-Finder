SNAP for Older Pennsylvanians

Pennsylvania's Department of Human Services (DHS) runs SNAP through county assistance offices, and it publishes a gross income ceiling well above the federal one. For older residents the more useful news is a short form: the Simple SNAP application, built for households where everyone is 60 or older or has a disability.

Pennsylvania and USDA figures here were read on November 15, 2026, and are the ones DHS lists as effective October 1, 2026.

Pennsylvania's higher income ceiling

Federal law sets SNAP's gross income test at 130 percent of the poverty line. Pennsylvania's published guideline, effective October 1, 2026, is higher because the state uses broad-based categorical eligibility (BBCE). USDA's state chart, updated November 12, 2026, lists Pennsylvania as making all households eligible through a pamphlet, with no limit on assets and a gross income limit of 200 percent of poverty.

DHS says households within these guidelines receive a TANF-funded brochure, Help for Pennsylvanians in Need. That brochure is the TANF-funded benefit USDA's chart refers to. DHS adds that household size, income and whether a member is 60 or older or disabled all shape the result, and that the surest way to learn your amount is to apply.

What BBCE means for savings and income

For an older Pennsylvanian with money in the bank, the most useful fact here is that there is no asset test under the DHS guideline. A one-person household with gross income at or below $2,660 a month is categorically eligible, so neither the federal net-income test nor the $4,750 resource limit is applied.

Eligibility still is not a payment. The benefit equals the maximum, $306 for one person, minus 30 percent of net income, so deductions decide how much arrives each month. Getting to net income starts with the $217 standard deduction for one to three people.

The medical deduction is the one older Pennsylvanians most often miss. Out-of-pocket costs above $35 a month that insurance does not cover, such as prescriptions, dentures, health insurance premiums and certain medical transportation, can be subtracted, and DHS lists medical expenses among the things to have ready when applying. Older households can also deduct shelter costs above half of their adjusted income with no cap. USDA's own worked example shows a two-person elderly household with $1,200 of Social Security and pension income, $300 in excess medical costs and $600 in shelter costs qualifying for $434 a month.

A household whose gross income is above the DHS guideline is not shut out. It can still apply under the regular federal rules, where a household with an elderly or disabled member only has to meet the net limit, $1,330 a month for one person and $1,804 for two in FY2027, and the $4,750 resource limit does apply.

The Simple SNAP application

DHS offers a shorter paper form, the Simple Application for Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, in English and Spanish. You can use it if everyone in the household is 60 or older or has a disability, the household buys and prepares food together, and nobody has earnings from work. People in a SNAP household do not have to be related, so two older friends sharing a kitchen can apply together.

Applying online gives a similar shortcut: DHS says that when you enter your details in COMPASS, the system presents a simplified application. Both versions are linked from the DHS SNAP for Older Adults page.

Three ways to apply in Pennsylvania

Online: COMPASS is the state benefits website and also has a “Do I Qualify?” screening tool.

In person: take a completed paper application to your county assistance office, where staff can walk you through it. The county assistance office directory shows the office for each county.

By mail: download the paper or Simple application and send it to the same county office.

The head of household, a spouse, another responsible member or a designated authorized representative may apply. That representative can be a friend, relative, neighbor or community group you trust to shop with your benefits. Before you start, gather birth dates and Social Security numbers for everyone, income details, housing costs and medical bills.

After filing, you can follow the case at trackmybenefits.pa.gov, which covers LIHEAP, Medicaid, SNAP and TANF applications. DHS warns that a new federal law requires some recipients to work, volunteer, or attend school or job training; USDA says households made up only of elderly or disabled members are exempt from work requirements.

Your ACCESS card and appeal rights

Approved households get a Pennsylvania ACCESS card and choose a PIN; at the register the PIN is checked electronically and the purchase is debited from the SNAP account. DHS recommends locking the card whenever it is not in use, to protect benefits from skimming. The Card Lock tool on its EBT vendor Conduent's ConnectEBT website and app can block every purchase, or only in-store purchases outside Pennsylvania, and an Auto Re-Lock setting closes the card again 30, 60 or 90 minutes after you unlock it. The free myCOMPASS PA app shows the EBT balance and transactions, eNotices and document uploads. Details are on the DHS EBT page.

If a county assistance office denies, cuts or stops your SNAP, you can ask for a fair hearing. SNAP is the exception to DHS's usual written-appeal rule, because a SNAP appeal can be made orally. You file it with the DHS office that took the action, within the time frame printed on the notice; USDA sets the SNAP limit at 90 days from the decision. The request goes to DHS's Bureau of Hearings and Appeals, where an Administrative Law Judge hears the case by phone or in person, whichever you prefer, and most recipient hearings are by phone. DHS says SNAP decisions typically come within 60 days of the appeal. After that you may ask the Secretary of Human Services to reconsider within 15 days, or petition Commonwealth Court within 30 days. The DHS hearings and appeals page explains each step.

Frequently asked questions

Is $2,660 the income limit for a single senior in Pennsylvania?

It is the maximum gross monthly income DHS publishes for a one-person household from October 1, 2026, matching the 200 percent BBCE limit on USDA's chart. Under it there is no asset or net-income test, but the benefit amount still depends on net income after deductions.

Can I use the Simple application if I still work part-time?

No. DHS limits the Simple SNAP application to households with no earnings from work where everyone is 60 or older or has a disability. Use the standard paper application or COMPASS instead.

Will money in savings count against me in Pennsylvania?

Not while gross income stays within the DHS guideline, because USDA's chart shows no asset limit for Pennsylvania's BBCE group.

Who can apply for my parent?

A designated authorized representative, such as a relative, friend, neighbor or community organization, can apply on their behalf and use the benefits to shop for them.
