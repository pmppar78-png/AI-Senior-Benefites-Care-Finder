SNAP Help for Older Adults in Reading

In Reading, SNAP is handled by the Berks County Assistance Office in the Reading State Office Building. It is the one county office for all of Berks, and the DHS directory lists LIHEAP heating contacts for it as well, which is useful because the two programs interact for older households.

Berks office lines and LIHEAP rules reflect DHS pages as published on November 15, 2026; SNAP limits apply from October 1, 2026.

Visiting or contacting the Berks office

The DHS directory places the Berks County Assistance Office in the Reading State Office Building, 625 Cherry Street, Reading, PA 19602-1188, open 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Its toll-free number is 1-866-215-3912 and its local line 610-736-4211. Heating assistance has numbers of its own: 610-736-4228 or 866-215-3911.

You can apply for SNAP in person on Cherry Street, mail a paper application there, or file through COMPASS.

How a Berks senior applies for LIHEAP

The 2026–2027 LIHEAP season opened November 2, 2026, and runs through April 23, 2027, according to the DHS LIHEAP page. LIHEAP pays a one-time cash grant, from $200 to $1,000 depending on household size, income and fuel type, straight to the utility or fuel dealer, and it does not have to be repaid.

A Reading resident can apply online through COMPASS or print the paper application and return it to the Cherry Street office. You do not have to be on public assistance, owe an unpaid heating bill, or own your home. DHS lists the yearly income limit as $23,940 for one person and $32,460 for two. Households facing a shutoff notice, broken heating equipment or less than a 15-day fuel supply may also qualify for a crisis grant.

For an older Berks household, that grant also matters for food help: the Pennsylvania SNAP guide describes how LIHEAP can raise the SNAP amount.

Berks County income lines

Money in the bank is not what decides a Berks case. As long as a household stays under the state's gross line, savings are not counted at all. DHS's gross line is $2,660 a month for someone alone and $3,608 for a two-person household. Payment still depends on net income, with $306 as the one-person maximum, and the state guide also covers the Simple application for older households.
