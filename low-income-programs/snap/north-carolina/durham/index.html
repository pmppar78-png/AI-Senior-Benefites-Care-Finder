A Durham Senior's Guide to Food and Nutrition Services

Durham County's Department of Social Services, which refers to itself as DCoDSS, gives applicants more ways to reach it than most counties. Its page lists online, fax, phone and in-person options for new applications and recertifications alike, and a mobile app for sending forms.

Information from DCoDSS and NCDHHS was gathered on November 15, 2026. Income lines and benefit amounts are the federal ones in force for FY2027.

Four routes for getting an application to DCoDSS

The DCoDSS Food and Nutrition Services page starts with ePASS. It also accepts faxed applications at 919-560-8501, and says it is temporarily taking applications by telephone at 919-560-8000. Paper forms can be picked up at the main DSS building, 414 E. Main Street, Durham, which is open 7:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., and dropped in boxes inside or outside the building.

The county tells applicants they do not need to know whether they are eligible before applying. DCoDSS reviews the application and makes the call.

Recertifying and reporting changes

Recertification has the same set of routes, plus the DSS Mobile App, which lets you submit a completed and signed recertification from a phone. Changes such as a new address or a rise in Social Security can be reported by email to FNSChanges@dconc.gov, by fax, through the app or in person.

For an older adult on a fixed income, the change worth reporting quickly is a new medical expense. Higher out-of-pocket medical costs can raise the benefit, but only once the county knows about them.

What Durham households need to know about limits

North Carolina's broad-based categorical eligibility means that, for most Durham households, there is no asset test. USDA's chart sets the gross income line at 200 percent of poverty, which for a Durham resident living alone sits at $2,660 a month and for a two-person household at $3,608, as set out in the North Carolina FNS guide.

Households above that line can still qualify under federal rules if their net income is low enough, but for them the $4,750 resource limit for a household with someone 60 or older or disabled does apply. The guide also explains the simplified program for SSI recipients 65 and older.

Frequently asked questions

Can I recertify Durham FNS from my phone?

Yes. Durham County DSS accepts completed and signed recertifications through its DSS Mobile App, as well as ePASS, fax, phone and in person.

Do I need to bring documents when I apply in Durham?

You can apply first. DCoDSS reviews the application and the caseworker will ask for whatever proof is needed.
