Food and Nutrition Services for Older Charlotte Residents

Mecklenburg County runs Food and Nutrition Services through its Department of Community Resources. One change long-time residents should know: the lobby of the Catherine M. Wilson Center, formerly the Kuralt Center, is closed to customers, so in-person help has moved to two community resource centers.

Mecklenburg County and NCDHHS pages were read on November 15, 2026; dollar figures are USDA's for FY2027.

Where Charlotte residents get in-person help

The county's Food and Nutrition Services page names two places to apply in person or drop off a finished application: the Ella B. Scarborough Community Resource Center at 430 Stitt Road, Charlotte, NC 28213, and the Valerie C. Woodard Community Resource Center at 3205 Freedom Drive, Suite 1000, Charlotte, NC 28208. Both also serve as points where SNAP participants can find food and grocery resources.

Applying online through ePASS avoids the trip altogether. Mecklenburg also takes applications over the phone: call 704-336-3000 to complete one.

Help for seniors who cannot shop

Mecklenburg spells out a step that matters for homebound older adults: a recipient who cannot shop for themselves can name someone they trust as an authorized representative by contacting the county's Customer Connection Call Center at 704-336-3000. That person can then use the EBT card to buy groceries on the recipient's behalf.

The county also points SNAP households to Double-Up Bucks, which matches SNAP dollars spent at participating farmers markets. There is nothing to enroll in; you bring the EBT card and receive tokens to spend at the market.

State rules, Charlotte figures

Charlotte cases follow North Carolina's broad-based categorical eligibility policy, which USDA lists with no asset limit and a 200 percent gross limit. Put in dollars, gross income can reach $2,660 a month for one person or $3,608 for two, and the North Carolina guide explains why those are the FY2027 lines. The most a household can receive is $306 for one person or $562 for two.

An SSI recipient 65 or older who buys and prepares food separately from others in the home may qualify for the state's simplified program instead. NCDHHS adds that married people living in the same home cannot be treated as separate households. The guide lists the program's three fixed amounts.

Frequently asked questions

Can I still apply at the Kuralt Center?

The county says the Catherine M. Wilson (formerly Kuralt) Center lobby is closed to customers. Use ePASS, the phone application, or one of the two community resource centers.

How do I let my daughter shop with my EBT card?

Ask Mecklenburg County to name her as your authorized representative through its Customer Connection Call Center.
