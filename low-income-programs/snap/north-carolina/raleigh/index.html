Food and Nutrition Services in Raleigh for Older Adults

In Raleigh, Food and Nutrition Services, North Carolina's name for SNAP, is handled by Wake County Health & Human Services, the department that houses the county's social services. The county steers most people to the online application, yet it also keeps paper routes open, which matters for older residents who prefer not to use a computer.

Wake County and NCDHHS pages were read on November 15, 2026. Dollar limits apply from October 1, 2026, through September 30, 2027.

Ways Wake County accepts an application

Wake County calls the online route the easiest way to apply, and it links straight to ePASS, the state portal. Its how-to-apply page also offers a downloadable paper form in English and Spanish that can be mailed, faxed or emailed to the county.

Three Raleigh buildings accept paper applications in person: Health & Human Services at Departure Drive, the Health & Human Services Clinics, and the Health & Human Services Swinburne Building. Regional centers in Cary, Fuquay-Varina, Wake Forest and Zebulon take them as well, so a relative living elsewhere in the county can drop off forms close to home.

Questions about a Wake County case go to 919-212-7000. The county keeps two email addresses for FNS: Foodandnutrition@wake.gov for applications and FNSrecert@wake.gov for households that already have an active case. Mailed forms go to Wake County Health & Human Services, P.O. Box 46833, Raleigh, NC 27620.

Keeping benefits once approved

Wake County's Food and Nutrition Services page asks households to watch their mail for recertification packets. It warns that paperwork not returned on time may mean filling out a brand-new application. For a senior living alone, asking a family member to keep an eye on DSS mail can prevent a gap in benefits.

People on SSI who are 65 or older may be moved into North Carolina's simplified program, which pays a set amount based only on housing cost and certifies cases for 36 months. NCDHHS converts eligible cases automatically when that amount is at least as high as regular FNS.

North Carolina rules that apply in Raleigh

North Carolina is on USDA's broad-based categorical eligibility chart with no asset limit and a 200 percent gross income limit, so savings rarely decide a Wake County case. In dollars, a Raleigh resident living alone can have up to $2,660 a month in gross income, and a couple up to $3,608, before that shortcut stops applying; the North Carolina FNS guide shows how those FY2027 lines were worked out. The most a single adult can receive is $306.

Above 200 percent, the federal net test and $4,750 resource limit for older households come back into play. The same guide covers deductions and the simplified program in depth.

Once benefits arrive, the State Farmers Market on Agriculture Street, which Wake lists as open every day of the year, is one place to use them: some of its vendors take SNAP/EBT, although Double Bucks is not offered there, according to the county's farmers market list.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I hand in a paper FNS application in Raleigh?

Wake County lists three Raleigh sites, Health & Human Services at Departure Drive, the Health & Human Services Clinics and the Swinburne Building, plus regional centers elsewhere in the county.

What happens if my recertification is late in Wake County?

The county says paperwork not received on time may mean you have to complete a new application, so return the packet by its due date.
