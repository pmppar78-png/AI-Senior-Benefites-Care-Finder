Applying for SNAP in Columbus After 60

A Columbus food-assistance case is handled by Franklin County Department of Job and Family Services. The county is not limited to one waiting room: it lists three Columbus opportunity centers and also runs a mobile unit for events in the community.

Franklin County JFS and Ohio Benefits material was read November 15, 2026.

Where Franklin County JFS serves residents

Franklin County JFS lists its Northland Opportunity Center, 1721 Northland Park Ave., Columbus, OH 43229, as the main office. It also lists the East Opportunity Center at 1055 Mount Vernon Ave., Columbus, OH 43203 and the West Opportunity Center at 314 N. Wilson Road, Columbus, OH 43204. Weekday hours are 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

The county's customer access number is 1-844-640-OHIO (6446). Use the menu for an existing case question rather than going to a center just to ask whether paperwork arrived.

Options when a trip downtown is difficult

Franklin County Faith Connections sends a mobile unit to churches, mosques, synagogues, temples and other faith-based sites. The county also offers a request form for the mobile unit, an online case-document upload and opt-in texts or automated calls for application, reporting and renewal reminders.

On its SNAP page, the county provides a pre-interview questionnaire for recertification. Completing it before the call may help an older adult gather medical, rent and income information in advance. Columbus residents 60 and older can also ask about the county's senior meal delivery program, which uses a sliding scale, by calling 614-525-6200. Paper verification can be faxed to Franklin County JFS at 614-233-2398.

Before the first Columbus purchase

Franklin County notes that, effective May 13, 2026, Ohio requires SNAP users to unlock their Ohio Direction Card before online or out-of-state purchases. Plan for that step before placing a grocery order for delivery. The Ohio senior SNAP guide explains income, BBCE and deductions once for all counties.

Starting online from Columbus

Franklin County applicants can file through Ohio Benefits before choosing an opportunity center.

Franklin County Office on Aging and Senior Options

The Franklin County Office on Aging is at 280 East Broad St., Room 300, Columbus, OH 43215. Its main line, (614) 525-6200, is staffed weekdays from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., and the same number reaches Senior Options from 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Adult Protective Services has its own line, (614) 525-4348, and the office lists Ohio Relay TDD at (800) 750-0750.

Senior Options is the place to ask about the county's senior meal delivery or help at home. It does not process SNAP, so keep the JFS case number separate when calling.

Worked example: a couple just over the gross line

Take two spouses, both 67, with $2,400 a month in combined Social Security. USDA's FY2027 gross limit at 130% of poverty for two people is $2,345, so they miss Ohio's BBCE route by $55. Because a member is 60 or older, the county can test them under regular federal rules instead: no gross test, a $1,804 net limit for two and a $4,750 resource limit.

Subtracting the $217 standard deduction leaves $2,183. The couple would therefore need at least $379 in combined medical costs above $35 and excess shelter costs to reach $1,804. Rent receipts, utility bills and pharmacy printouts are what make that case, so bring them to the interview.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to visit the Northland office to apply in Columbus?

No. Ohio Benefits accepts online applications, and Franklin County lists additional centers, document upload and a community mobile unit.

What changed for Ohio Direction Card online purchases?

Franklin County says users must unlock the card before online or out-of-state purchases, effective May 13, 2026.

Can a Columbus couple over 130% of poverty still get SNAP?

Possibly. With a member 60 or older, Franklin County can apply the regular federal net-income test of $1,804 for two people and the $4,750 resource limit.
