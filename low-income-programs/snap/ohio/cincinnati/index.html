SNAP and Food Assistance in Cincinnati for Older Adults

Hamilton County Job and Family Services is the Cincinnati agency for SNAP, which the county calls food assistance. Its site groups food help alongside Medicaid, cash assistance and Adult Protective Services, so a family caregiver can find several services in one place.

Hamilton County JFS pages were read November 15, 2026; general Ohio rules link to the state guide.

Contacting Hamilton County JFS

Hamilton County JFS food assistance lists 1701 Patricia McCollum Way, Cincinnati, Ohio 45237, with phone 513-946-1000 and hours Monday through Friday, 7:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The page also gives hamiltoncountypublicassistance@jfs.ohio.gov as its public-assistance email. Avoid sending a full Social Security number in an ordinary email; use the state portal or ask the county how to send sensitive documents securely.

Applications can begin through Ohio Benefits, with case questions directed to the county. Its food-assistance page describes the program as help for families and individuals stretching their food budgets.

County notes that matter to seniors

Hamilton County says healthy people under 60 take part in Food Assistance Employment and Training while receiving benefits. Someone 60 or older should still read any notice describing a work rule and ask what exemption applies. The county also warns that criminals can drain an unprotected SNAP benefit account.

The county home page describes Elder Protection, through Adult Protective Services for elderly residents. Families concerned about neglect, exploitation or a vulnerable person's safety can use that channel; SNAP staff decide only food-assistance eligibility.

Worked example: over the line on paper, not in practice

Hamilton County's page notes that some households over its income limit can qualify when someone is elderly or disabled. Here is how that plays out for a 66-year-old living alone on $1,900 a month in Social Security. That is above the 130% figure of $1,729, so Ohio's BBCE route does not apply.

Regular federal rules drop the gross test for her. Take off the $217 standard deduction and $1,683 remains, against a one-person net limit of $1,330. Medical spending above $35 and the uncapped shelter deduction have to close a $353 gap. A Medicare premium, pharmacy costs and a rent bill above half her adjusted income often do it.

A threshold to verify with the caseworker

Hamilton County's page mentions 200% of poverty; USDA's chart, updated November 12, 2026, lists Ohio at 130% with no asset limit. The Ohio SNAP guide explains that older households can also qualify under regular federal rules.

Filing without the drive to Patricia McCollum Way

The state self-service portal lets a Hamilton County household start SNAP from home.

Council on Aging of Southwestern Ohio

Hamilton County's Area Agency on Aging is the Council on Aging of Southwestern Ohio, 4601 Malsbary Road, Blue Ash, OH 45242. Its Aging and Disability Resource Center takes calls weekdays from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. at (513) 721-1025 or toll-free (800) 252-0155; select option 2. Staff there explain services for older adults and caregivers and do initial eligibility screenings for Council programs.

People already enrolled in the Elderly Services Program in Hamilton County have a direct line, (513) 592-2800. Ask the call center about help with meals or home care while JFS works on the food-assistance application.

Frequently asked questions

What does Hamilton County call SNAP?

Its site uses the name food assistance and explains that the federal program is SNAP, loaded on an Ohio Direction Card.

Does Hamilton County JFS handle elder abuse reports?

Its home page lists Elder Protection through Adult Protective Services for elderly residents, separate from the food-assistance decision.

Who is the Area Agency on Aging for Cincinnati?

Its headquarters are in Blue Ash, and the agency is called the Council on Aging of Southwestern Ohio. Its call center is (513) 721-1025, option 2, weekdays 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.
